**No**, Laredo is **not** considered part of the Lower Rio Grande Valley (the RGV or "The Valley").  
While Laredo sits along the Rio Grande and is located in South Texas, the term **Lower Rio Grande Valley** specifically refers to the four-county region at the southernmost tip of Texas:

* **Cameron County** (Brownsville, Harlingen)  
* **Hidalgo County** (McAllen, Edinburg, Pharr)  
* **Starr County** (Rio Grande City, Roma)  
* **Willacy County** (Raymondville)

### **Key Differences**

| Feature | Lower Rio Grande Valley (RGV) | Laredo Area |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Region/Classification** | Lower Rio Grande Valley / "El Valle" | Middle Rio Grande / Laredo Borderlands / South Texas Plains |
| **Primary County** | Cameron, Hidalgo, Starr, Willacy | Webb County |
| **Geography** | Low-lying coastal delta/floodplain | Brush country and semi-arid plains |
| **Distance** | Far southern tip along the Gulf coast | \~140 miles upriver to the northwest of the RGV |

### **Common Points of Confusion**

* **Area Code (956):** Both Laredo and the RGV share the 956 telephone area code, leading outside visitors to lump them together.  
* **Historical Name:** Laredo was briefly the capital of the short-lived *Republic of the Rio Grande* (1840), but modern regional definitions separate Laredo's trade-and-logistics hub from the agricultural and delta region of the RGV.
