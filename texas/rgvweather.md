While both **McAllen** and **Laredo** feature warm, semi-arid, sub-tropical climates with mild winters, key differences emerge in their winter weather patterns due to location and proximity to the Gulf of Mexico:

## **Key Differences Summary**

| Weather Metric (Winter Avg) | McAllen (Lower Rio Grande Valley) | Laredo (Mid-Rio Grande Borderplex) |

## **Main Weather Drivers**

### **1\. Temperature & Overnight Cooling**

* **McAllen:** Sits closer to the coast and further south, keeping both daytime highs and nighttime lows roughly **3°F to 5°F warmer** than Laredo throughout December and January.  
* **Laredo:** Located further inland and upriver, daytime temps are mild, but dry continental air allows heat to escape quickly after sunset. Lows regularly drop into the mid-40s during cold fronts.

### **2\. Humidity & Dew Point**

* **McAllen:** Frequently receives maritime tropical air blowing in off the Gulf of Mexico, making winter mornings feel more humid or overcast with morning fog.  
* **Laredo:** Features significantly lower average winter dew points. Winter days in Laredo tend to feel drier and crisper.

### **3\. Vulnerability to Polar Fronts ("Nortenders")**

* Both cities experience strong arctic cold surges (*norte* fronts) moving down through Texas, which can temporarily drop temperatures by 30°F–40°F in a few hours.  
* Because **Laredo** is further north and further inland, those cold air masses arrive stronger and last slightly longer, bringing a higher frequency of light freezing temperatures. By the time cold fronts reach **McAllen**, they are moderated slightly by coastal maritime influences.

### **4\. Precipitation**

* Winter is the driest season for both locations, but **McAllen** generally receives slightly more ambient winter rainfall/drizzle (averaging around 0.8–1.0 inches per month) compared to **Laredo** (averaging 0.5–0.7 inches per month).
